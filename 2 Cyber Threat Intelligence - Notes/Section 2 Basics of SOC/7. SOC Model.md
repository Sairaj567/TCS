## Detailed Notes

### Lecture Flow

We just had a look at the typical activities that a SOC is typically carrying out.
the model that most SoCs are operating with.
And typically most SoCs operate with a so-called three tier model, meaning at the very bottom we have automation.
Automation is typically dealing with commodity malware, dealing with very repetitive tasks.

### And good automation mimics the steps that an analyst would do

And good automation mimics the steps that an analyst would do.
For easy cases, but it would never.
Just isolate machines or disabling users and that's it.
We want to get more efficient.
We want to increase the performance, which leads to the fact we try with automation to mimic the steps of an analyst.

### Then we have the tier one

Then we have the tier one.
I mentioned it already.
We have a three tier SoC in most cases, and tier one would again deal with commodity malware, which would essentially result in the fact that they deal with easier tasks that can or should not be automated for whatever reason.
And if we look at the distribution of alerts, in this case, we would roughly have a distribution of 70% of alerts that would be handled by tier one and automation.

### Then we have tier two

Then we have tier two.
Tier two is in most cases responsible for dealing with more advanced malware.
They also deal with harder tasks in terms of doing SOC work and looking at the distribution, we would probably deal with something like 25% of alerts and the general idea is that whenever tier one is not capable of investigating the alert to the fullest extent, or whenever they already know that it's going to be a high severity alert, they escalate to tier two.
tier two is really, the next step in the process.
If tier one knows already, we cannot deal with this either because the severity is so high or because we don't know what we are actually facing.

### And then we have tier three

And then we have tier three.
those guys should be the most advanced in most SoCs.
They typically deal with something like 5% of alerts, if at all.
I also saw numbers that were way lower than that.
and their entire purpose is to deal with anything that one and two are not capable of solving, but also doing proactive threat hunting and advanced forensics, meaning the tier three will proactively surf through your logs and try to identify threats that were not uncovered by your [[2 Cyber Threat Intelligence - Notes/Section 8 MITRE and ATTACK/50. Detections|detections]].

### And they will also do advanced forensics in case you have a breach or yo

And they will also do advanced forensics in case you have a breach or you have an alert on an incident, to identify how the adversary could come in and how you could actually get better over time.
This is, again, not necessarily transferable to any enterprise and any SoC out there, but this is what most SoCs are doing.
And again, the distribution of alerts.
This is also highly specific across the various SoCs.
But the general consensus is that those numbers represent what most SoCs are actually doing.
