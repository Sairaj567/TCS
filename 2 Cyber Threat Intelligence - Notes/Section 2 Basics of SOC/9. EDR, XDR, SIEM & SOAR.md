first of all, we have EDR.
That one stands for endpoint detection and response.

### And the entire idea of an EDR is to do behavior monitoring for your endp

And the entire idea of an EDR is to do behavior monitoring for your endpoints.
The Microsoft equivalent to that one is the defender for endpoint and a few sentences on EDR.
Traditionally, what we had from a security perspective to secure endpoints and servers, for example, was [[2 Cyber Threat Intelligence - Notes/Section 2 Basics of SOC/7. SOC Model|malware]] scanning tools.
These were tools that used hashes, signatures and all that to identify potentially malicious files on your operating system.

### And again, the issue with that is that you play a constant catch up game

And again, the issue with that is that you play a constant catch up game because you need the latest signatures, you need the hash values of the latest malware.
And this is something that does not scale efficiently because new malware gets developed all the time.
It's super easy to change hash values and all that stuff, which is why the security community and the vendors, yeah, had an alignment in the sense that we need something better than that.
And that is where EDR was born.

### And instead of looking at static values like hashes or leveraging signat

And instead of looking at static values like hashes or leveraging signatures, we talk about behavior monitoring.
Meaning we talk about the entire behavior on the endpoint.
any malicious processes, any malicious files, any malicious behavior from users is something that is in scope of EDR.
And the entire purpose of EDR is to detect this malicious behavior and to provide us with alerts on this.

### Then we have XDR

Then we have XDR.
That is the same concept as EDR.
But instead of focusing on endpoints only like we do with this one, we extend this to the rest of the IT infrastructure, which is why it's called extended detection and response.
You can think of this again as behavior monitoring beyond the endpoint.

### And then the Microsoft ecosystem

And then the Microsoft ecosystem.
We have a service called defender XDR and also defender for [[2 Cyber Threat Intelligence - Notes/Section 3 AZURE/11. Cloud Computing Properties|cloud]].
And they do what defender for endpoint is doing for other parts of the IT infrastructure, such as for your exchange online environment.
For your [[1 Cybersecurity Foundation - Notes/5 – Data Security and Encryption|Azure]] resources with defender for cloud.
But also maybe for your on premises Active Directory domain controllers.
This is also something that you can do.

### Then we have a SIM solution and a SIM

Then we have a SIM solution and a SIM.
First of all stands for Security Information and Event Management.
And the purpose of a SIM is first of all, to centrally collect, correlate and analyze what we do with our logs in the Microsoft ecosystem.
This is [[2 Cyber Threat Intelligence - Notes/Section 16 Case Study III - Leveraging CTI in Microsoft Sentinel/98. What is Microsoft Sentinel|Sentinel]].
Sentinel is a cloud native SIM that you can deploy in Azure and ingest logs from anywhere to correlate the logs, and then get incidents from Sentinel.

### The entire purpose of a SIM is to act as a single point of class for you

The entire purpose of a SIM is to act as a single point of class for your SoC.
the idea is you have a lot of [[2 Cyber Threat Intelligence - Notes/Section 8 MITRE and ATTACK/49. Data Sources|data sources]].
This can be EDR, XDR as well, by the way, but also your on premises firewalls, your proxy, your NetFlow, your endpoints.
And you take all these logs, you correlate them and by correlating them you identify alerts and incidents that your SOC is then working on.
SIM is really maybe the most important tool in a SoC, but certainly.
One of the most important ones.

### And then we have so-ah

And then we have so-ah.
This is security, orchestration, automation and response.
Think of this as a way to automate your [[1 Cybersecurity Foundation - Notes/1 – Introduction to Cybersecurity|incident response]] procedures.
And again good automation is not replacing humans.
Good automation is instead mimicking the steps that a SoC analyst would take, to simply react more efficiently and in a more timely manner on commodity malware and easy incidents.

### In the Microsoft ecosystem
