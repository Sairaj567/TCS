## Detailed Notes

### Lecture Flow

let's talk about the indicator lifecycle in the context of CTI.
And what this is, is think of CDI that arrives in the form of a CDI report.
for example a company or an organization gets breached and then the forensics team arrives, or maybe already present because they in-house, whatever.
a report is filed, that contains the latest ttps of the adversaries.

### And then also IOCs are gathered

And then also [[2 Cyber Threat Intelligence - Notes/Section 6 Cyber Threat Intelligence(CTI)/34. IOCs and IOAs|IOCs]] are gathered.
And what happens is the report, reveals the respective indicators and therefore the IOCs.
And then those IOCs are leveraged, meaning they transfer over from a revealed state to a mature state because organizations can now start to leverage those IOCs.
for example, in the breach, where a report was written about, the forensic teams gathered domains, IP addresses, whatever, therefore they revealed it.
we can leverage those domains and IP addresses in our [[2 Cyber Threat Intelligence - Notes/Section 8 MITRE and ATTACK/50. Detections|detections]] to see whether the adversary also targeted us.
that means whenever it's transferred over to a mature state, we can start to discover those IOCs in our infrastructure, which means this is already transferring then over to utilized state, meaning that organizations can now leverage the indicators in their own infrastructure to see whether they have been targeted by the adversaries as well.

### And from there, we always go back to reveal state, because we also, cont

And from there, we always go back to reveal state, because we also, continuously try to analyze those indicators.
That's it for the, life cycle, at least.
As you can see, it's pretty straightforward.
It's all about leveraging what is coming up in the reports and transferring that over to your daily SoC operations.
By just leveraging the IOCs and making sure that you discover the respective indicators in your organization as well.
